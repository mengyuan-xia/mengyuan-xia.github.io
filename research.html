Consumer Mobility and Electric Vehicle Adoption: Evidence from China

with Ce (Matthew) Shi

Full paper coming soon

This paper asks whether travel to EV-leading megacities promotes electric vehicle (EV) adoption in lower-tier Chinese cities. We combine city-level vehicle registrations with intercity travel data and use a shift-share instrument based on pre-sample travel patterns and job postings in EV-leading megacities to estimate the effect of travel exposure on EV sales. We find that a 1% increase in travel exposure to these megacities raises local EV sales by 1.42%, with particularly pronounced effects for new EV brands. To quantify the implications of consumer mobility for preferences and policy outcomes, we estimate a random-coefficients demand model (BLP). Counterfactual analyses show that eliminating the mobility channel reduces aggregate EV sales by 26.90% and substantially lowers consumer surplus. Mobility also complements EV subsidies: the absolute sales response to subsidy removal is 37% larger under observed mobility than without mobility exposure. Our findings highlight mobility-driven spillovers as a demand-side factor that promotes the spatial diffusion of EVs and amplifies the effectiveness of subsidies.

Extreme Cold Acts as a Behavioral Barrier to Electric Vehicle Adoption

with Ce (Matthew) Shi, Kai Fang, and Sirui Zhu

Working paper · September 2026

Full paper coming soon

Abstract

Mitigating climate change requires the rapid diffusion of low-carbon technologies, yet climate change itself may alter the climate conditions under which these technologies perform. Electric vehicles (EVs), a key pathway for decarbonizing the transport sector, experience substantial performance degradation under extreme temperatures, but whether these physical constraints translate into real-world consumer behaviour remains largely unknown. Here, we combine monthly vehicle registration data from 318 Chinese cities with high-frequency weather observations to examine how temperature affects EV adoption. We show that one additional day with a daily mean temperature below −10°C reduces monthly EV sales by 1.3%, whereas sales of internal combustion engine vehicles remain unaffected. The negative effect is significantly larger for lower-range EVs, in historically warmer regions, and in smaller cities with less charging infrastructure. Together, these findings are consistent with heightened consumer awareness of EV performance, rather than general avoidance behaviour, driving the observed decline in adoption. Applying the estimated temperature-sales relationship to CMIP6 climate projections further shows that future warming generally alleviates the behavioural barrier imposed by extreme cold while generating substantial spatial heterogeneity across cities. Our findings highlight the need for climate-differentiated policy designs that combine charging infrastructure with credible information, in support of transport decarbonization under climate change.

Protecting Workers, Replacing Workers? Inclusive Labor Policies and Automation Adoption

with Da Fang, Yichun Lu, and Qihang Xue

Working paper · September 2026

Latest version (PDF)

Abstract

This paper investigates how migrant-inclusive labor policies affect firms' technology choices. We construct a novel, multidimensional city-year inclusiveness index and combine it with firm-level panel data. Using a dialect-network instrumental variable strategy, we find that migrant inclusion policies significantly accelerate robot adoption. Decomposing the policy index reveals that the compliance-cost channel drives this automation response: provisions expanding public-service access and labor-rights protection raise the cost and rigidity of formal employment, pushing firms toward labor-saving capital. Conversely, administrative burden relief acts as a mitigating factor. Furthermore, we find that this capital-labor substitution is concentrated among firms with high labor intensity, limited financial capacity, and strict legal enforcement. Our findings demonstrate that when inclusive policies transform flexible migrant labor into heavily regulated employment, firms substitute machines for labor, highlighting a critical trade-off between labor formalization and workforce displacement.
